The BQ algebra at the Dirac level

At the Dirac level the BQ algebra lives in 4×4 complex matrices, in the Weyl representation. Each of its sixteen basis elements is a 2×2 block matrix whose blocks are BQ-Pauli elements, so every Dirac-level product is a block product of BQ-Pauli products. Only the four vectors βμ are defined by hand; the other twelve elements are their products. All coordinates stay real.

The Pauli rotor U doubled into the Weyl boost Λ = diag(U−1, U), acting on vectors, bilinears and triples by one similarity.

Lorentz transformations

Choose a four-vector and a velocity and see its boost aL = ΛaΛ−1, with Λ = [[U−1, 0], [0, U]] = e−ψαI/2 the double of the Pauli rotor U. 4Gravity, as local rapidity

The rapidity of the Lorentz boost becomes a field χ(r, t): the same doubled rotor, now a different boost at every point. The Galilean coframe gives the metric, read at the Dirac level as the Clifford square and as the anticommutator of the curved β-matrices.

Dirac step 9 · gravity as a local rotor

The gravity rotor at the Dirac level

Newtonian, Hubble and azimuthal rapidity fields feed the BQ-Pauli rotor G, and its Dirac double Qg = diag(G−1, G) = e−χα/2 gives the exact image dRg = QgdRQg−1, whose square is flat. Its Galilean limit θ gives the metric as the Clifford square θθ = ds²𝟙, and gμν as the anticommutator of the β-matrices θμ, where the exact dRg,μ give the flat g⁰μν. With the riverbed, the boosted basis, the comparison with Schwarzschild, de Sitter and Kottler, and a 4×4 check.

Reproduce in Python

The folder dirac/py_sources holds an independent SymPy implementation. Each script builds the 4×4 basis from the BQ-Pauli blocks and checks every product exactly along two routes: as a block product through the BQ-Pauli table, and as an ordinary 4×4 complex matrix product.

  • 1 · The basis
  • bq_dirac_table.py · the 16 × 16 table, Pauli block content, Clifford relations, χ, trace orthogonality, block route = 4×4 route
  • bq_dirac_representations.py · Weyl and Dirac representations: the basis in the Dirac representation, the matrix S with XD = SXWS−1, the identical table, S = e−(π/4)β̃0, and the vector and boost in both
  • 2 · Vectors and their products
  • bq_dirac_bilinear.py · the channels of AB, the block form with −ATB, all 144 pairs, and ∂A, ∂G, UU, ∂R, ∂∂ from the papers
  • bq_dirac_triple.py · the triple rule, ∂ on a general even field, the block form with −D(ATB), Maxwell and the Lorentz force
  • bq_dirac_quadratics.py · the field square and the sandwich, the Pauli blocks FF and FTAF, the EM invariants and stress–energy array, the fluid products, and a boost along I
  • 3 · Lorentz transformations
  • bq_dirac_lorentz.py · Λ = diag(U−1, U) = e−ψαI/2 as the double of U, the boost of a vector and its blocks, the Dirac lemma, boosts along any n, rotations, and the composition of two boosts with the Thomas–Wigner angle
  • bq_dirac_bilinear_lorentz.py · the boost of C = AB as ΛCΛ−1, the block form with U(ATB)U−1, the channel rule, the invariants, the complex form and the Pauli correspondence
  • bq_dirac_triple_lorentz.py · the boost of E = D(AB) as ΛEΛ−1, both sectors as four-vectors, the invariants, the Pauli rule U−1EU−1, and the four-gradient on a general boosted field
  • bq_dirac_lorentz_representations.py · Lorentz transformations, Weyl and Dirac: ΛD = SΛWS−1 in blocks and as a rotor, the commuting square, the same boosted coordinates, the Dirac block mixing, spinors and Λ2, and Λ = e(ψ/2)σI in both
  • bq_dirac_gravity_rotor.py · the gravity rotor at the Dirac level: Qg = diag(G−1, G) = e−χα/2 and the nested rotor Qg,φQg,r, the exact image dRg with the Pauli image as its block, its flat square, the boosted basis, θθ = ds²𝟙, and {dRg,μ, dRg,ν} = 2g⁰μν𝟙 against {θμ, θν} = 2gμν𝟙, both time orientations

Conventions used throughout

The Dirac level is the BQ algebra in the Weyl representation, built from the BQ-Pauli blocks 1̂, T̂ = i1̂, Î, Ĵ, K̂ and σ̂I, σ̂J, σ̂K: β0 = [[0, T̂], [T̂, 0]] and βi = [[0, K̂i], [−K̂i, 0]], so that β0² = −𝟙 and βi² = +𝟙.

The other elements are products: αi = β0βi, ΣI = βJβK (cyclic), the pseudoscalar χ = −β0βIβJβK and the pseudovectors β̃μ = χβμ. Standard-Model gamma-matrix conventions and General-Relativity index raising are not used.

Each model computes its results from the explicit matrices and shows a numerical check; the Python sources repeat every product as an exact symbolic check. The BQ definitions in the papers remain authoritative.
