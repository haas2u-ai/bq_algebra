BQ Weyl and Dirac Representations

← The BQ algebra at the Dirac level

The BQ-Dirac algebra has one set of sixteen basis elements and one multiplication table, but it can be written in more than one set of 4×4 matrices. The BQ construction is done in the Weyl representation; the Dirac representation is needed for massive particles and for the comparison with the standard Dirac formalism. One real orthogonal matrix S carries the one into the other. This page starts with S and why both representations are used, and then shows the sixteen basis matrices and all 256 products in either representation, with one switch.

The transformation matrix S, from Weyl to Dirac

Why we start with Weyl, and when Dirac is needed

Weyl: the construction representation

  • The Pauli level stays visible. A Weyl vector is a = [[0, A], [−AT, 0]]: its two blocks are the BQ-Pauli minquat A = a0T̂ + a·K̂ and its time adjoint. A product of two vectors is then ab = [[−ABT, 0], [0, −ATB]]: the Pauli bilinear ATB sits in it as a block.
  • Grade and block go together. The even grades (𝟙, αi, Σi, χ) are block diagonal and the odd grades (βμ, β̃μ) block off-diagonal.
  • The Pauli rotor is the boost. Λ = diag(U−1, U), so the Dirac Lorentz transformation of a vector follows in one line from the Pauli one, AL = U−1AU−1. The same holds for the gravitational rotor: the PG metric, the flows and the CL invariants are computed here.
  • The chirality is diagonal. χ = diag(T̂, −T̂): the two blocks are the two Weyl sectors, and the massless equations decouple into them.

This is why every BQ-Dirac model on this site is built in the Weyl representation: every Dirac result contains the Pauli result as a block (de Haas 2026, Weyl-level EM, the construction-oriented representation).

Dirac: the application representation

  • Mass. In the Weyl representation PP = EE factorizes as (P − E)(P + E) = 0, and taken on their own the factors give nontrivial solutions only for m0 = 0. Massive fermions need spinors and a representation that mixes the parity and time-reversal duplexes: the Dirac representation (de Haas 2025, BQ Dirac Gravity, eqs. 68–76).
  • Time on the diagonal. β0 = diag(T̂, −T̂), so a vector is [[a0T̂, a·K̂], [−a·K̂, −a0T̂]]. At rest the spinors separate into an upper and a lower block, u(0) ∝ [φ; 0] and v(0) ∝ [0; η]: positive- and negative-energy adapted sectors.
  • Comparison. With βμ = iγμ, the BQ-Dirac βμ are i times the standard Dirac-representation γ matrices (axis order I, J, K = z, y, x), so the massive Dirac equation, its non-relativistic limit and the literature are read in this form.
  • The spinor boost is constructed. The Lorentz operator of Dirac spinors is SΛS−1, the Weyl boost carried over by S, not a postulated matrix.

The working order is therefore Dirac → Weyl → Dirac: take the massive spinor to the Weyl representation with S−1, do the geometric calculation there, and return with S (de Haas 2025, M2(ℂ) unification, Fig. 6).
