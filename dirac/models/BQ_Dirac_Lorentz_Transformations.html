BQ-Dirac Lorentz Transformations

At the Pauli level a boost along Î acts on a four-vector from both sides, AL = U−1AU−1, and its time adjoint goes the other way, (AL)T = UATU. The Dirac vector a holds A and AT as its two off-diagonal blocks. One block-diagonal matrix Λ = diag(U−1, U) therefore moves both at once, by an ordinary similarity. That Λ is the double of U, and it is itself an element of the algebra: the exponential of the boost bivector αI. From there the page boosts vectors, reads the boost on the basis, turns to other directions and rotations, and composes two boosts into a boost and a Thomas–Wigner rotation. Vectors only.

The doubling in short

  • Two rules, one similarity. The Pauli boost U−1AU−1 is not a similarity, and the time adjoint needs its own rule UATU. In a = [[0, A], [−AT, 0]] both sit together, and Λ = diag(U−1, U) acts on them as ΛaΛ−1.
  • Λ is in the algebra. αI = diag(σI, −σI) carries σI with opposite signs in its two blocks, so e−ψαI/2 = diag(e−ψσI/2, eψσI/2) = diag(U−1, U). The Pauli rotor and its inverse are the two halves of one even element.
  • Products follow. Since Λ−1Λ = 𝟙, every product transforms by the same similarity: (ΛaΛ−1)(ΛbΛ−1) = Λ(ab)Λ−1. 𝟙 commutes with Λ, so the scalar of ab and the square aa are invariant.
  • One group. Boosts e−ψn·α/2 and rotations e−θm·Σ/2 are all Λ = diag(M, (M†)−1): M Hermitian for a boost, unitary for a rotation. Their products are the even rotors of the Weyl algebra.

Sources and status

The Pauli rules, the rapidity and the Lemma on basis–component compatibility follow the BQ-Pauli papers; Λ = diag(U−1, U) and the similarity XL = ΛXΛ−1 follow de Haas (2026), BQ-Weyl electromagnetism, eqs. 161–196. The paper writes the rotor as U = exp((ψ/2)Î) in eq. 161; that is the phase diag(eiψ/2, e−iψ/2). Its matrix Λ in eq. 163 needs U = exp((ψ/2)σI), which this page uses.

The Pauli papers align the frames along I before boosting (Synge’s communal photon). The exponential form shows what that alignment buys: along I, U is diagonal and the Doppler factors e±ψ sit on single matrix entries. The transformation rule itself holds in any direction, with the Hermitian Un = eψn·σ/2, as section 4 shows. This is an extension of the papers.
