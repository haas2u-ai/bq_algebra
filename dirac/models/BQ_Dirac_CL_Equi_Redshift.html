BQ-Dirac CL as an Equi-Redshift Flow

The constant-Lagrangian (CL) condition DΓ/Dt = 0 is stated at the rapidity level, with Γrotor = cosh χr cosh χφ; in the Galilean-coframe metric its slow-flow counterpart is γrs, the river’s Lorentz factor relative to the static Killing observers (the BQ-Pauli page CL in the metric). At the Dirac level both are the same Clifford operation: the product of two unit time vectors. That product is the square of the rotor between the two observers; its scalar part is the γ factor, its bivector part the direction of the relative velocity.

usu = −c²γrs(𝟙 − (vr/c)αr − (vφ/c)αφ) = −c² e−χrs n·α,   β̂0β0 = −e−χeff m·α,  cosh χeff = cosh χr cosh χφ = Γrotor

CL then says, at both levels, that the rotor between the static and the river observer keeps its rapidity along the flow: an equi-redshift flow. The two levels differ only in how the radial and the azimuthal motion combine: the rotor composes the rapidities relativistically, the metric adds the river velocities in the Galilean way. Units: c = 1, rs = 2GM/c² = 1. Radial river vr = √(2GM/r) − Hr (paper chart, Hubble term only when switched on); Keplerian azimuthal river vφ = √(GM/r); CL river vφ = √(K − vr²) for r ≥ R, rigid inside R.

γrs along the disk

γrs − 1 is the fractional clock slowing of the river relative to the static observers. With the Keplerian azimuthal river it falls as r grows; the CL river keeps it constant beyond R: the river flows along an equi-redshift level.
