The eight basis matrices

Matrices as defined in the BQ-Pauli slides (de Haas, 24 September 2026). Every product on this page is computed live from these entries; nothing is looked up except the check against the paper's table.

What the table shows

  • Squares. Every space-time element squares to −1̂ (T̂² = Î² = Ĵ² = K̂² = −1̂); every spin-norm element squares to +1̂.
  • Quaternion products. ÎĴ = −ĴÎ = K̂, ĴK̂ = −K̂Ĵ = Î, K̂Î = −ÎK̂ = Ĵ: the spatial K̂i anticommute and close on themselves.
  • Pauli products. σ̂Iσ̂J = −K̂, σ̂Jσ̂K = −Î, σ̂Kσ̂I = −Ĵ: two spin-norm elements give a space-time element, with a minus sign in cyclic order.
  • Mixed products. K̂iσ̂i = σ̂iK̂i = T̂ on the diagonal; off the diagonal K̂iσ̂j = −σ̂jK̂i lands on ±σ̂k.
  • T̂ is central. T̂ = i1̂ commutes with everything: T̂σ̂i = K̂i and T̂K̂i = −σ̂i. Multiplying by T̂ moves an element between the two bases.

Reading the colours

Because T̂ = i1̂ and K̂i = iσ̂i, each space-time element is i times its spin-norm partner. Which basis a product lands in depends on the pair:

  • space-time · space-time: K̂iK̂j (i ≠ j) stays in the space-time basis; the squares give −1̂ and T̂K̂i gives −σ̂i, both in the spin-norm basis;
  • spin-norm · spin-norm: σ̂iσ̂j (i ≠ j) gives ∓K̂k in the space-time basis; the squares give +1̂;
  • mixed: T̂σ̂i = K̂i and K̂iσ̂i = T̂ land in the space-time basis, K̂iσ̂j (i ≠ j) in the spin-norm basis.

So the space-time basis alone does not close under multiplication: T̂T̂ = −1̂ and T̂K̂i = −σ̂i leave it, and the spin-norm basis is needed to span the full biquaternion algebra.

Method, as in the reproduction appendix: use the explicit matrices, execute the multiplication, decompose the result on the basis, and compare with the stated table.
