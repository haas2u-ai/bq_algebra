BQ Gravity Rotor: from Rapidity to Metric

Gravity enters the BQ-Pauli algebra as a local Lorentz rotor field. Rapidity potentials feed the rotor G(r, t) = eχσ̂/2: at every point it boosts the coordinate line element, dRG = G−1dR G−1, where a Lorentz transformation would use one global rapidity. The exact image has a flat quadratic: a local Lorentz transformation alone gives back Minkowski space, and so does every consistent slow-flow truncation of it. The gravitational content is in the choice of a Galilean coframe with a Lorentzian quadratic: the slow-flow coframe θ0 = c dt, θr = dr − v dt, squared with the Minkowski signature, gives curved Einsteinian metrics, exactly Schwarzschild with its horizon for v = √(2GM/r). The Einstein tensor then shows which riverbeds are vacuum. Choose the rapidities that feed the rotor.

A field of Lorentz transformations

  • Global versus local. A Lorentz transformation relates two inertial frames with one constant rapidity. Here χ(r, t) is a spacetime field, so G(r, t) assigns a boost to every point. A constant χ gives back the global boost of the Lorentz pages.
  • Two exact projections. On the reference vector cT̂ the rotor gives the gravitational four-velocity Ug = G−1(cT̂)G−1 = c cosh χ T̂ − c sinh χ Î, with γ = cosh χ and vFR = c tanh χ < c. On dR it gives the exact image dRG.
  • Where the gravity enters. The exact quadratic (dRG)TdRG = dRTdR is flat, and so is every consistent truncation of dRG. The metric comes from a Galilean coframe with a Lorentzian quadratic: (1) the slow-flow, Galilean limit of the rotor action, θ0 = c dt and θr = dr − v dt with v = cχ, and (2) the Minkowski-signature quadratic of that coframe. The coframe carries the physics; its Lorentzian quadratic gives the Einsteinian metric.
  • Sign of the flow. With G = eχσ̂r/2 and dRG = G−1dRG−1, the matrices give θr = dr − cχrdt and gtr = −vr, as in the paper’s coframe and metric (eqs. 46, 51). For vr > 0 this is the outgoing PG chart, and for the Hubble term a contracting one: the time reverse of infall plus expansion. The time-orientation toggle flips the rotor rapidity to −χ and gives the ingoing, expanding chart, gtr = +vr. Both charts describe the same geometry.

Two boosts

With a radial and an azimuthal rapidity the boosts are applied one after the other, radial first: dRG = Gφ−1(Gr−1dR Gr−1)Gφ−1. Each step keeps dRG a minquat, and the exact quadratic stays flat. The single product G−1dRG−1 with G = GφGr is not the same: GφGr is not a pure boost (it contains the Thomas–Wigner rotation), and that product leaves the minquat space at order χrχφ. The order of the two boosts and the Wigner rotation only affect terms of order χrχφ, which the slow-flow projection drops.
