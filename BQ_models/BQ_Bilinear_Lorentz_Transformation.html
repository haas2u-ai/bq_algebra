BQ Lorentz Transformation of the Bilinear

The bilinear C = ATB = c11̂ + cK·K̂ + cσ·σ̂ of two four-vectors is boosted along Î by boosting both factors. The time adjoint AT transforms as UATU and B as U−1BU−1, so the inner pair cancels and C transforms by a similarity, CL = UCU−1, with the rotor U = eψσ̂I/2. Choose two four-vectors and a velocity.

Why a similarity, and what stays invariant

  • Vectors and bilinears transform differently. A four-vector is boosted from both sides with the same factor, AL = U−1AU−1. In the bilinear the adjoint brings the opposite factor, (AL)T = UATU, so (AL)TBL = UAT(UU−1)BU−1 = UCU−1.
  • Invariant channels. 1̂, T̂, Î and σ̂I commute with σ̂I, so c1, the T̂ channel and the longitudinal components cK,I, cσ,I are unchanged. Ĵ, K̂, σ̂J, σ̂K anticommute with σ̂I and mix in the pairs (Ĵ, σ̂K) and (K̂, σ̂J).
  • Invariant combinations. The complex three-vector 𝒞 = cσ + icK is rotated by a complex orthogonal matrix, so 𝒞·𝒞 = (cσ² − cK²) + 2i cK·cσ is invariant. For the EM field these are B² − E²/c² and E·B/c. For two plain four-vectors cK·cσ = (a×b)·(a0b − ab0) = 0.
  • The time adjoint. With CT = c11̂ + cK·K̂ − cσ·σ̂ (as on the field-quadratics page), (CL)T = U−1CTU = (CT)L−1, the same reversal as for vectors.

Direction of the boost

As for the vectors, the rule UCU−1 with a diagonal U holds for boosts along Î. A boost in another direction is first rotated into Î; a rotation R acts on the bilinear as RCR−1 as well, so every bilinear transforms by a similarity under the full Lorentz group.
