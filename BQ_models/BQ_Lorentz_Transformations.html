BQ Lorentz Transformations

A Lorentz boost along Î acts on a four-vector A = a0T̂ + a·K̂ from both sides with the same rotor, AL = U−1AU−1, where U = eψσ̂I/2 = diag(eψ/2, e−ψ/2) and ψ is the rapidity. The inverse boost is UAU, and the time adjoint AT transforms the opposite way. Choose a four-vector and a velocity.

Vector and boost

The rotor

Because σ̂I² = 1̂, the exponential series splits into even and odd powers: eψσ̂I/2 = cosh(ψ/2) 1̂ + sinh(ψ/2) σ̂I. With γ = coshψ and βγ = sinhψ, the rapidity is ψ = artanh β and e±ψ = γ(1 ± β), the relativistic Doppler factor.

In the (a1, a0) plane

AAL = U−1AU−1AL−1 = UAUa0² − a1² = constlight cone

The boost slides the point along its hyperbola: the invariant a0² − a1² does not change, and the light-cone coordinates a0 ± a1 are multiplied by e∓ψ. The coordinates a2, a3 across the boost are unchanged.

Check by explicit matrix multiplication

Why both sides, and why the adjoint reverses

  • Basis transformation. U−1T̂U−1 = coshψ T̂ − sinhψ Î and U−1ÎU−1 = coshψ Î − sinhψ T̂, while Ĵ and K̂ are unchanged. The coordinate rule a0′ = γ(a0 − βa1), a1′ = γ(a1 − βa0) is the same transformation read on the coordinates instead of the basis: K̂μΛνμ = U−1K̂νU−1.
  • In the matrix. A = [[i(a0 + a1), a2 + ia3], [−a2 + ia3, i(a0 − a1)]]: the boost multiplies the diagonal entries by e−ψ and eψ and leaves the off-diagonal entries alone.
  • The time adjoint. Since U is real and diagonal, (AT)L = U−1ATU−1 = (UAU)T = (AL−1)T and (AL)T = UATU. The adjoint of a boosted vector is boosted the other way.
  • Invariance and covariance. Hence (AL)TBL = UATU U−1BU−1 = U(ATB)U−1: every bilinear transforms by a similarity, and its 1̂ and T̂ channels are invariant, so ATA is the same in every frame.

Direction of the boost

The rule U−1AU−1 with a diagonal U holds for boosts along Î. Frames moving in another direction are first rotated so that their relative velocity lies along Î (Synge’s communal photon); a boost in another direction can always be replaced by one rotation and one Î-boost, which is where Thomas–Wigner rotation enters.
